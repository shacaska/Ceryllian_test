How to play

You are Sabbialt, an infiltrator slipping into the island city of Ceryllian. Between you and the next district stands the Lamplighter, a watchman with a hooked lantern-pole. Wound him 4 times to win. Run out of cards in your hand and he finishes you.

The street

1You ›34‹ Him67
  • The street is 7 spaces. You start on space 2, he starts on space 5.
  • Distance is how many spaces apart you are. Side by side is distance 1.
  • Your blades reach distance 1. His hook is deadliest at distance 2, so the fight is about getting inside his reach without being punished on the way in.
  • You can't share a space or pass each other, except with Slip Past or Blur.

Reading him

  • His deck sits face-down. The back of the top card shows his intent: ▲ Press, ■ Hold or ▼ Withdraw. That is all you know when you plan.
  • Each card of his does two things, one per beat. Press cards tend to come at you, Hold cards keep their distance and wait, Withdraw cards back off.
  • A tell drawn in red means that card has turned to its Nightmare side, usually faster and nastier.
  • Under his card, "Left" counts how many of each tell are still in his deck. The Cards tab lists everything he can do.

A round

  1. Look at his tell.
  2. Put a card from your hand into Beat 1 and Beat 2. Each card is either played, or spent to catch your breath (see Stamina).
  3. Commit. His card flips face up.
  4. Resolve Beat 1, then Beat 2. In each beat the faster action goes first: Swift, then Normal, then Slow. If you're the same speed, he goes first.
  5. A strike that wounds cancels the other side's action that beat, if it hasn't happened yet. A Swift cut stops a Slow swing.

The red spaces on the street show where his next strike would land from where he stands now.

Range and facing

  • "Strike 1" hits only at distance 1. "Strike 2" hits only at exactly distance 2. "Strike 1–2" hits at either.
  • At the start of each round you both turn to face each other. Moving doesn't turn anyone unless the card says so.
  • Strikes only hit in front, unless the card says "on either side".

That makes Slip Past special. Step past him at distance 1 and you turn to face his back, but he doesn't turn. His strikes miss you for the rest of the round, and yours can still land.

Stamina: your hand is your life

  • You start with 9 cards in hand. Every card you play goes to your fatigue pile, and you never reshuffle.
  • Catch your breath. Instead of playing a card, you can spend it. If he doesn't hit you in that beat, take a different card back from your fatigue pile. Every beat needs a card while you still have one.
  • Breathe takes 2 cards back, then returns to your hand itself. A successful Parry or Riposte takes 1 back.
  • If your hand is empty at the end of a round, he finishes you.

Getting hurt

  • When his strike hits you, the card in that beat is lost for good, whether you played it or spent it.
  • Desperation. Each time you lose a card, turn one of your other cards to its stronger Exert side. It stays turned. If it was in your fatigue pile, it comes back to your hand.

Hurting him

  • Each wound you deal turns a card in his deck to its Nightmare side at the end of the round. You'll see the red tell when it reaches the top.
  • Heavy Cut and Sundering Cut deal 2 wounds. Everything else deals 1.

Two more things

  • Study takes your whole round: your other beat stays empty, and you see his next card before you plan the next round.
  • Alarm. Some of his Withdraw cards signal the watch if you aren't right next to him. Three alarms and you lose.

Tips

  • Catch your breath when his tell says he's backing off.
  • Adjacent to him on a ▲ Press? Slip Past, then Quick Cut.
  • Losing a card hurts, but Desperation makes your next cards hit harder. Choose which card to turn with the next few rounds in mind.
Designer notes

How this app rules the gaps

  • Parry, Riposte and Ghost Step cover their whole beat, even when he goes first in a tie.
  • A card that moves and strikes does both parts, in order.
  • Your Beat 1 card is already in your fatigue pile when Beat 2 resolves, so a card taken back in Beat 2 can be it.
  • A card turned to Exert before it's played resolves on its Exert side.
  • When there's only one possible pick, the app makes it for you.

On the table

  • Your cards are printed on both sides: flip one over to exert it. Keep your fatigue pile face up.
  • His cards can't be double-sided, because the back carries the tell. The Watchman half and the Nightmare half share one face, upside down to each other, with the tell at both ends of the back. Turning a card means rotating it 180°.
